What is Breast Cancer

Breast cancer is a disease in which abnormal cells in breast tissue grow and divide in an uncontrolled way. These cells can form a tumour and, in some cases, spread to other parts of the body.

Breast cancer is not one single disease. There are different types and subtypes, and they can behave differently. Treatment is therefore based on the individual's specific diagnosis.

Breast Tissue

The breast contains glands, ducts, fatty tissue, connective tissue, blood vessels and lymphatic vessels.

Awareness

Knowing what is normal for you can help you notice changes that should be discussed with a healthcare professional.

Signs & Symptoms

Breast cancer may cause no symptoms in its early stages. When changes do occur, they can include:

Changes in Breast size

One breast may suddenly change in size or shape.

Breast or Nipple Pain

The Breast or Nipple may feel painful, sore or uncomfortable.

Swelling

Part or all of the Breast may become swollen of feel bigger.

Changes in skin colour

The breast skin may become red, darker or look bruised.

Important: If you notice a new or unusual breast change, speak with a qualified healthcare professional. Not every breast change is cancer, but it should be properly assessed.

Risk Factors

Breast cancer risk can be influenced by a combination of genetic, biological, lifestyle and environmental factors.

Age

The risk of breast cancer generally increases with age.

Family History

A strong family history of breast or ovarian cancer can increase risk.

Inherited Genes

Certain inherited gene changes, including BRCA1 and BRCA2, can increase risk.

Lifestyle Factors

Alcohol consumption, physical inactivity and some other lifestyle factors can influence risk.

Breast Density

Higher breast density can increase risk and may affect mammography.

Hormonal Factors

Certain hormonal and reproductive factors can affect breast cancer risk.

Screening & Diagnosis

1

Clinical Assessment

A healthcare professional may discuss symptoms, medical history and examine the breast.

2

Mammogram

Mammography uses X-rays to examine breast tissue and may be used for screening or diagnosis.

3

Ultrasound

Ultrasound uses sound waves to examine breast tissue and can help evaluate a lump.

4

Biopsy

A biopsy removes a small sample of tissue so that it can be examined in a laboratory.

Treatment Methods

Treatment depends on the type and stage of breast cancer, tumour characteristics, overall health and individual preferences.

🏥

Surgery

Surgery may remove the tumour or the entire breast, depending on the diagnosis.

☢️

Radiation Therapy

Uses high-energy radiation to destroy or control cancer cells.

💊

Chemotherapy

Uses medicines that circulate through the body to destroy or control cancer cells.

🧬

Targeted Therapy

Targets specific features of cancer cells.

🌸

Hormone Therapy

May be used for cancers that are sensitive to hormones.

🛡️

Immunotherapy

Helps the immune system recognise and attack selected types of cancer.

Prevention

Not all breast cancers can be prevented. However, some lifestyle choices may help reduce risk.

  • 🎀 Stay physically active.
  • 🎀 Maintain a healthy body weight.
  • 🎀 Limit or avoid alcohol.
  • 🎀 Avoid smoking.
  • 🎀 Follow recommended screening guidelines.
  • 🎀 Discuss strong family history with a healthcare professional.
Remember: Healthy living can reduce some risks, but it cannot guarantee that breast cancer will not develop.

Support & Coping

A breast cancer diagnosis can affect physical health, emotional wellbeing, relationships, work and finances.

Build a Support Network

Family, friends, support groups and healthcare professionals can provide emotional and practical support.

Ask Questions

Write down questions before appointments and ask your healthcare team to explain unfamiliar terms.

Take Care of Yourself

Rest, stay active when able and communicate concerns or treatment side effects to your care team.

Seek Emotional Support

Counsellors, psychologists, social workers and support groups can help people cope.
